PHASE P1 — POLITICAL CAREER ENTRY
Goal

This phase answers one question:

How does a completely new player become interested in politics?

If this phase fails...

Nothing else matters.

Design Philosophy

Politics should feel like entering an existing world.

Not creating one.

When a player joins...

The country already has:

Government
Opposition
Economy
News
Political scandals
Elections
Businesses
Media

You are entering history.

Not starting history.

First Login

The player creates a character.

Not a politician.

Just a citizen.

Entering the World

Instead of spawning in a 3D city...

The player enters the National Career Portal.

This is the hub for all careers.

The screen says:

Choose your path

Politics
Business
Journalism

Civil Service

The player selects Politics.

Welcome to Politics

A mentor-style introduction appears.

Not a long tutorial.

Something like:

Politics is built on trust, organization, and leadership.

You currently have no supporters, no organization, and no public influence.

Your first goal is to become known.

Starting Stats

Every new political player begins with:

Recognition: 0.5%

Trust: Neutral

Supporters: 0

Volunteers: 0

Political Capital: 0

Funding: Small starter amount

Organization: None

Office: None

Party: None

This ensures everyone starts from the same political baseline.

First Objective

The game does not say:

Win an election.

Instead it says:

Become Publicly Known

Progress bar:

Recognition

0 / 10%

That's the first milestone.

How Do You Gain Recognition?

The player receives invitations to real events occurring in the country.

Examples:

Attend a town hall.
Visit a school.
Observe a city council meeting.
Join a public debate.
Volunteer during flood relief.
Speak at a university discussion.
Meet local business owners.

These are not scripted forever—they come from the living simulation.

The First Five Actions

To avoid overwhelming new players, only five actions are initially available.

Attend Event
Meet Citizens
Read News
Talk to Mentor
View Political Landscape

Everything else unlocks naturally.

Political Landscape Screen

This is one of the first screens every player should see.

It answers:

Who governs the country?
Which parties exist?
When is the next election?
What are today's biggest issues?
Which regions support which parties?
Current approval ratings.
Major ongoing debates.

Players immediately understand the country's situation.

The First Session

A typical first session might look like:

Read the morning briefing.
Attend a university discussion.
Gain a few supporters.
Receive a message from a journalist.
Unlock the "Organize Community Meeting" action.
Reach 2% recognition.
End session.

The player feels genuine progress without needing to found a party immediately.

Daily Retention

Why return later?

Because the world has changed.

When the player logs back in:

A new policy debate is trending.
Another politician criticized your statement.
Citizens reacted to your appearance.
An organization invited you to speak.
Public recognition increased overnight.

The world moved while you were away.

Deliverables of Phase P1

By the end of this phase we have defined:

How a player enters politics.

The political onboarding experience.
Starting stats.
First objectives.
First actions.
The initial political UI.
The motivation for returning to the game.

I would also make one architectural decision now:

Politics should never be locked behind grinding.

The early game should be about learning the country's political landscape and building a public identity, not collecting arbitrary experience points. Every action in the first hours should either teach the player something about the world or meaningfully increase their visibility and connections. That keeps the opening engaging while naturally leading into the next phase: building a real political organization.
