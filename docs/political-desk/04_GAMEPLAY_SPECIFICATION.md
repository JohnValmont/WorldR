The Long-Term Gameplay Loop

Here's the loop I think can keep players engaged for months:

Morning Login
        ↓
Read HQ Briefing
        ↓
Handle Urgent Issues
        ↓
Review Party Departments
        ↓
Approve or Reject Proposals
        ↓
Attend One or Two Public Events
        ↓
Respond to Media
        ↓
Plan Tomorrow's Priorities
        ↓
Log Out

Notice what's not in that loop:

Grinding XP.
Clicking the same button 100 times.
Collecting daily rewards.

The reward is that your political organization keeps growing, the world reacts to your choices, and the next time you log in, there are new opportunities and new problems waiting.

One Final Change Before We Continue

After designing P5, I think we need to split the UI design from the gameplay design.

Right now we're mixing systems and interface.

From this point forward, I'd recommend:

P6–P12: Design mechanics only (elections, parliament, government, coalitions, campaigns, diplomacy).
After all mechanics are finished: Create a dedicated Political UI/UX Design Series where we design every screen, button, popup, layout, notification, and navigation flow using the finished mechanics as the foundation.

That's the same order most successful management games follow: systems first, interface second. It prevents redesigning dozens of screens every time a mechanic changes.

Good. This is the phase that will make or break the game.

Everything before this was setup.

P5 is where the player actually starts playing.

And before I write anything, I want to establish one design principle that I think will define WORLDr.

⚠️ The GearCity Principle

Ask yourself:

Why can someone spend 500 hours in GearCity?

Not because there are 500 buttons.

Because every button changes the future of your company.

Politics should feel exactly the same.

Every click should make the player think:

"If I do this today, what happens six months from now?"

Not:

"+2 popularity"

🚨 First Major Architecture Decision

We need to decide:

What type of election system does WORLDr use?

This cannot be vague because every UI, every button, every gameplay mechanic depends on it.

Real countries use different systems:

India → First Past the Post (543 constituencies)
Germany → Mixed Member Proportional
UK → First Past the Post
Netherlands → Proportional Representation
France → Two-round
Indonesia → Open List PR

Since WORLDr will eventually have 75+ countries, every country should use its own constitutional election system.

That means:

India feels like India.
Germany feels like Germany.
Japan feels like Japan.

The election engine supports multiple systems instead of forcing every country into one model.
